What do historical postcards reveal about Australia's places?

Explore historical postcards from the National Museum of Australia (NMA), the State Library of New South Wales (SLNSW), and Ansichtskarten Online (AKON), an Austrian National Library archive. Explore how these collections represent places and classify postcard imagery.

CHAPTER TWO / THE MATERIAL

More than a
picture on a card.

THE QUESTION How does cataloguing shape visual history?

Catalogue formats and labels influence how archives can be compared.

INSIDE THE ARCHIVE Single cards, grouped folders and visual-style labels do not describe equivalent units.

One catalogue record isn't always one postcard.

SLNSW includes 602 Single card and 582 Postcard records, alongside folders and grouped items. A folder can contain several cards even though it appears as a single record.

A CLOSER COMPARISON

Two ways to read colour.

The heatmap compares colour classifications within SLNSW formats. The stacked bars compare colour across three archives.

Colour varies with catalogue format.

In SLNSW, Colour accounts for 42% of Single card records and 49% of Folder records.

Different archives, different colour stories.

Colour accounts for 40.3% at SLNSW, 20.2% at NMA, and 22.0% at AKON.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

These are harmonised catalogue labels, not direct evidence of historical printing methods. Comparing proportions does not make the source descriptions equivalent.

WHAT WE LEARNED Catalogue descriptions shape the comparisons we can make.

Do Australian archives represent the same places?

Among 98 name-matched NSW localities, Sydney has a greater share of NMA depicted records, while Newcastle and Albury have greater shares of SLNSW mapped records. The 15 largest percentage-point gaps are shown; three places are labelled directly. Hover for exact counts.

HOW TO READ THIS COMPARISON

NMA uses 1,425 distinct NSW depicted-location records. SLNSW uses 1,036 mapped NSW catalogue records. Each percentage uses its own archive's NSW subset. Name-matched localities may require geographic verification.

Most AKON records lack these three subject tags.

04 / THE FINAL POSTMARK

The story of Australia
is also the story of what survives.

01

Representation is uneven.

New South Wales dominates the geographic subsets of both Australian collections. Among 330 NMA NSW depicted-place names and 244 SLNSW NSW locality names, 98 match after normalisation; shared places can still have different record shares.

02

A record is not always a card.

Some catalogue entries describe folders or groups. Record counts and physical postcard quantities are not interchangeable.

03

Archives select what becomes visible.

Name matches, mapped places and subject tags are distinct kinds of archival evidence. SLNSW and AKON can be mapped as points, while AKON’s Building, Mountain and Water tags describe a separate type of metadata.

REMEMBER THIS Archives reveal what survived, not everything made, sent or seen.
