A country in postcards.

What do Australia's historical postcards reveal about the places remembered, and those less visible in archives?

Explore Australia's places, catalogue formats, and visual culture through the National Museum of Australia and the State Library of New South Wales (NSW). Then consider an international perspective through AKON, the Austrian National Library’s historical postcard archive.

HOW TO READ THIS COMPARISON

NMA percentages use 1,425 distinct records linked to depicted NSW locations; SLNSW percentages use 1,036 NSW mapped catalogue records. A percentage point is the difference between two percentages. One NMA record may refer to several places, and standardised locality matches should be checked for geographic equivalence.

HOW TO READ THE RIBBONS

The flows count NMA spatial-relationship entries, not unique physical postcards or journeys between states. A single NMA record can have several places or relationship types. The smallest states and less-common relationship types are grouped as labelled “Other” categories to keep this diagram readable.

What appears in international postcard metadata?

AKON’s Building, Mountain and Water tags describe selected subject features. The UpSet plot shows how the tags overlap, which combinations are common, and how many records contain none of these three selected tags.

View chart specification

AKON TAGS, NOT AUSTRALIAN SUBJECT COUNTS

The Building, Mountain and Water indicators come from AKON’s subject metadata. They are not measured by matching fields in NMA or SLNSW, so this chart remains a standalone international example. A record without these tags may still depict other subjects; an absent tag does not mean a postcard lacks a subject.

WHAT WE LEARNED NMA and SLNSW emphasise shared NSW places differently. NMA’s relationship metadata reveals how Australian places are catalogued; the SLNSW–AKON map and AKON subject tags demonstrate other institutional perspectives.

04 / THE FINAL POSTMARK

The story of Australia
is also the story of what survives.

01

Representation is uneven.

New South Wales dominates the geographic subsets of both Australian collections. Among 330 NMA NSW depicted-place names and 244 SLNSW NSW locality names, 98 match after normalisation; shared places can still have different record shares.

02

A record is not always a card.

Some catalogue entries describe folders or groups. Record counts and physical postcard quantities are not interchangeable.

03

Archives select what becomes visible.

Name matches, mapped places and subject tags are distinct kinds of archival evidence. SLNSW and AKON can be mapped as points, while AKON’s Building, Mountain and Water tags describe a separate type of metadata.

REMEMBER THIS The archive shows us what was preserved and catalogued—not everything that was made, sent or seen.

COLOPHON / METHODS

Behind the
postcards.

Sources, processing choices and limits of interpretation. The three institutions are the sources of the underlying metadata; the visual storytelling and derived datasets were prepared for this project.

01 — Collection sources +

National Museum of Australia (NMA). Josef Lebovic Gallery collection no. 1; 3,805 cleaned item records. NMA Collection API

State Library of New South Wales (SLNSW). Miscellaneous Australian Postcards, Boxes 1–7 and 12; 1,559 catalogue records. SLNSW Box 3 catalogue example

Austrian National Library. AKON Historical Postcards; 34,846 cleaned records. AKON dataset and terms

02 — Maps & processing +

Australian state boundaries: Australian States GeoJSON , converted to TopoJSON. World boundaries: Natural Earth .

The Chapter 3 world map uses SLNSW geocoded NSW localities and AKON's mapped international place summaries, both displayed as points. NMA has no reliable point coordinates in this dataset, so its state-assigned spatial metadata appears separately in the alluvial diagram. The connected dot plot compares 98 name-matched NSW places in NMA and SLNSW; ambiguous matches require manual review.

03 — Reading these numbers +

All counts describe records in particular museum or library collections. A grouped catalogue entry may represent multiple cards, and an NMA record may link to multiple depicted places. The different denominators must not be combined.

Colour categories are harmonised from institution-specific fields, and AKON provides a binary colour flag. In Chapter 3, NMA geographic relationship roles and AKON subject tags represent different metadata concepts and cannot be compared directly. No chart measures historical postcard production, tourism, circulation or actual printing methods.

The downloadable data notes and summary-file index describe the supplied extracts and denominators. Collection totals describe this project snapshot, not current institutional holdings.

Chapter 3's connected dot plot uses 98 NSW place names matched after normalisation. The matched names are not necessarily verified equivalent sites. The new alluvial diagram counts NMA spatial-relationship entries, not distinct postcards; its source-to-role ribbons do not represent physical travel or changes over time.
