Postcards of Australia

Places, Collections and Visual Culture

Before digital photographs and social media, postcards offered a way to document places, share experiences and preserve memories.

Today, historical postcard collections provide glimpses of Australian landscapes, cities and cultural heritage. Yet the surviving records do not represent every place equally.

This visual story explores how Australia appears in two historical postcard collections: the National Museum of Australia (NMA) and the State Library of New South Wales (SLNSW).

We begin with geography, explore catalogue formats and visual styles, then compare these findings with AKON, an international collection from Austria.

Part Two — Collections and Visual Culture

How Australian Postcards Are Recorded

A postcard collection contains more than images. Its catalogue records describe formats, visual characteristics and groups of related materials. These descriptions influence the patterns we can discover from historical collections.

How SLNSW Postcards Are Catalogued

Individual cards dominate the recorded formats, with 602 Single card records and 582 Postcard records. However, the collection also includes folders, booklets and grouped materials.

This distinction matters because one catalogue record may describe several physical postcards. Explore the different record scopes to understand how the collection is organised.

Source: SLNSW, Miscellaneous Australian Postcards. The treemap covers 1,559 catalogue records. Rectangle area represents the number of catalogue records in each format.

Some grouped records contain multiple postcards. Treemap area therefore does not represent the exact number of physical postcard items.

Format and Visual Style

Visual-style classifications vary across SLNSW formats. Around 42% of Single card records and 49% of Folder records are classified as Colour.

However, these percentages depend on how many records belong to each format. Filter out smaller format groups before comparing their visual-style distributions.

Source: SLNSW. Each heatmap percentage uses the total catalogue records within its format as the denominator. The colour legend spans 0% to 100%.

Unknown denotes missing or uncertain visual-style classification. Percentages from very small format groups require cautious interpretation.

Visual Style Across Collections

The Australian collections differ in their recorded colour classifications. SLNSW has a Colour share of 40.3%, compared with 20.2% in NMA.

AKON, our international reference collection, records a Colour share of 22.0%. This comparison raises an important question: how much do institutional cataloguing practices shape the differences we observe?

Click a legend category to compare the same recorded visual style across all three collections.

Sources: NMA, SLNSW and AKON. Bars show the percentage of records within each collection. Every bar totals 100%.

Visual-style categories were harmonised from different institutional metadata. AKON uses a binary colour classification. Its Non-colour records are grouped under Monochrome / toned for this comparison.

Classification differences limit direct comparison. These figures do not establish the prevalence of colour in historical postcard production.

Part Three — International Perspective

Looking Beyond Australia

Australian collections reveal a geographically concentrated record of the past. To place this in a broader context, we examine AKON, a historical postcard collection from the Austrian National Library.

AKON is not a representative global sample. Instead, it demonstrates how an international collection can have its own geographic and historical biases.

Postcard Places Beyond Australia

Unlike the Australian collections, AKON covers places across numerous countries. Yet its records are heavily concentrated in Austria and other European countries.
