What a collection shows
is not everything that existed.

Postcards preserve glimpses of places and lives. But an archive reflects what was collected, catalogued and retained. A crowded spot on the map means more records in this collection—not necessarily more postcards produced there.

We follow the evidence from Australian states to individual places, then examine how cards are described. A European collection offers a final reminder that the institution itself shapes the story.

CHAPTER TWO / THE MATERIAL

More than a
picture on a card.

The formats and labels attached to postcards change how we compare them—and what we think we see.

THE QUESTIONHow does cataloguing shape visual history?
INSIDE THE ARCHIVESingle cards, grouped folders and visual-style labels do not describe equivalent units.↗
FIG. 05TREEMAP · SLNSW

One catalogue record isn't always one postcard.

SLNSW includes 602 Single card and 582 Postcard records, alongside folders and grouped items. A folder can contain several cards even though it appears as a single record.

EXPLORESelect a record scope to examine how catalogue formats are distributed.

05 / METHOD Rectangle area represents catalogue record counts among 1,559 records—not physical postcard quantities. A group's size is a count of descriptions.

FIG. 06HEATMAP · SLNSW

Colour varies with catalogue format.

Around 42% of Single card records and 49% of Folder records are classified as Colour. The label is part of the institution's metadata, not a universal description of historical printing.

EXPLOREUse the format-size dropdown to exclude small categories that yield unstable percentages.

06 / METHOD Every cell's percentage uses its own format as the denominator. The sequential legend spans 0–100%. Unknown is a metadata classification.

FIG. 07100% STACKED BAR CHART · THREE COLLECTIONS

Different archives, different colour stories.

40.3% of SLNSW records are classified Colour, against 20.2% at NMA and 22.0% at AKON. Comparing the labels is useful—assuming they mean precisely the same thing is not.

EXPLOREClick a legend category to highlight that visual-style group across collections.

07 / METHOD Percentages are calculated separately within each collection. Source labels were harmonised for comparison; AKON's binary Non-colour category is grouped under Monochrome / toned.

04 / THE FINAL POSTMARK

The story of Australia
is also the story of what survives.

01

Representation is uneven.

New South Wales dominates the examined Australian holdings, with visible concentrations at both state and locality scales.

02

A record is not always a card.

Some catalogue entries describe folders or groups. Record counts and physical postcard quantities are not interchangeable.

03

Labels carry institutional choices.

Colour categories and dated coverage differ between collections, limiting historical generalisation.

REMEMBER THISThe archive shows us what was preserved and catalogued—not everything that was made, sent or seen.

COLOPHON / METHODS

Behind the
postcards.

Sources, processing choices and limits of interpretation. The three institutions supplied the underlying metadata; the visual storytelling and derived datasets were prepared for this project.

01 — Collection sources +

National Museum of Australia (NMA). Josef Lebovic Gallery collection no. 1; 3,805 cleaned item records. NMA Collection API ↗

State Library of New South Wales (SLNSW). Miscellaneous Australian Postcards, Boxes 1–7 and 12; 1,559 catalogue records. SLNSW Box 3 catalogue example ↗

Austrian National Library. AKON Historical Postcards; 34,846 cleaned records. AKON dataset and terms ↗

02 — Maps & processing +

Australian state boundaries: Australian States GeoJSON ↗, converted to TopoJSON. World boundaries: Natural Earth ↗.

SLNSW mapped localities are high-confidence title-derived matches, not exact photographic coordinates. Hexbin cells were precomputed using an Australian equal-area projection and exported as geographic polygons. The NMA depicted-place analysis uses spatial metadata relationships.

03 — Reading these numbers +

All counts describe records in particular museum or library collections. A grouped catalogue entry may represent multiple cards, and an NMA record may link to multiple depicted places. The different denominators must not be combined.

Style classes were harmonised from institution-specific fields; AKON uses a binary colour field. Exact AKON dates exclude approximate or inferred periods. No finding measures historical postcard production, tourism, popularity or postcard circulation.

No third-party historical postcard photographs are reproduced on this page; the opening illustration is original CSS artwork.
