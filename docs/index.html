Australia, one
place at a time.

First, zoom out to state boundaries. Then follow the postcards into cities, towns and regional landscapes.

THE QUESTIONWhich Australian places are most visible in these collections?
THE BIG PICTURENew South Wales dominates the mapped records in both Australian collections.

Two collections. One dominant state.

About 68% of NMA's state-assigned counts and 87% of SLNSW's are linked to New South Wales. The difference reflects each collection's coverage rather than Australia's overall postcard history.

FROM STATESTO INDIVIDUAL PLACES ↓

Sydney leads—but the story spreads inland.

Sydney has the most high-confidence mapped SLNSW locality records, followed by Newcastle and Albury. Smaller towns appear across the state, beyond the capital's familiar views.

When nearby places become a cluster.

Individual symbols overlap in densely catalogued areas. Equal-area hexagons bring neighbouring records together, revealing concentrations without placing every locality on top of another.

The museum's most depicted places.

In NMA's records, Sydney (138) leads, followed by Mount Gambier (85) and Adelaide (63). Famous landmarks appear alongside cities and natural sites.

WHAT WE LEARNEDGeography in an archive is a map of representation—not a map of everywhere postcards existed.
02
CHAPTER TWO / THE MATERIAL

More than a
picture on a card.

The formats and labels attached to postcards change how we compare them—and what we think we see.

THE QUESTIONHow does cataloguing shape visual history?
INSIDE THE ARCHIVESingle cards, grouped folders and visual-style labels do not describe equivalent units.

One catalogue record isn't always one postcard.

SLNSW includes 602 Single card and 582 Postcard records, alongside folders and grouped items. A folder can contain several cards even though it appears as a single record.

Colour varies with catalogue format.

Around 42% of Single card records and 49% of Folder records are classified as Colour. These project categories summarise catalogue descriptions; they do not identify historical printing techniques.

Different archives, different colour stories.

40.3% of SLNSW records are classified Colour, against 20.2% at NMA and 22.0% at AKON. Comparing the labels is useful—assuming they mean precisely the same thing is not.

WHAT WE LEARNEDCollections preserve images, but their descriptions shape every comparison we make.

CHAPTER THREE / AN INTERNATIONAL LENS

The world beyond
our postmark.

AKON offers a European-centred reference point—not a universal record of global postcard history.

THE QUESTIONWhat changes when we look through another institution's collection?
THE CONTRASTA multinational collection can still be strongly concentrated in one region.

Beyond Australia, but centred on Europe.

AKON documents places across many countries. Its geography, however, is concentrated around Austria and other European locations, reflecting the perspective of a particular holding institution.

Austria stays first as rankings shift.

Austria ranks first across the selected decades of exactly dated AKON records. Other countries change positions, revealing shifts in the composition of the dated holdings—not in national postcard production.

What the dated timeline can—and cannot—tell us.

The series records 1,100 postcards for 1909, then 445 in 1919. World War I began in 1914, but this chart alone cannot identify the causes of the change.

Where buildings, mountains and water overlap.

More than half of AKON records contain none of these three selected tags. When they do appear, the intersections are usually smaller than single-tag groups. A missing tag does not imply a postcard has no subject.

WHAT WE LEARNEDEven a collection with worldwide locations is shaped by institutional history and incomplete metadata.

COLOPHON / METHODS

Behind the
postcards.

Sources, processing choices and limits of interpretation. The three institutions supplied the underlying metadata; the visual storytelling and derived datasets were prepared for this project.

01 — Collection sources +

National Museum of Australia (NMA). Josef Lebovic Gallery collection no. 1; 3,805 cleaned item records. NMA Collection API

State Library of New South Wales (SLNSW). Miscellaneous Australian Postcards, Boxes 1–7 and 12; 1,559 catalogue records. SLNSW Box 3 catalogue example

Austrian National Library. AKON Historical Postcards; 34,846 cleaned records. AKON dataset and terms

02 — Maps & processing +

Australian state boundaries: Australian States GeoJSON, converted to TopoJSON. World boundaries: Natural Earth .

SLNSW mapped localities are high-confidence title-derived matches, not exact photographic coordinates. Hexbin cells are supplied as precomputed geographic polygons. The original preparation notes describe an Australian equal-area grid; the projection identifier and generation script are not included in this extract. The NMA depicted-place analysis uses spatial metadata relationships.

03 — Reading these numbers +

All counts describe records in particular museum or library collections. A grouped catalogue entry may represent multiple cards, and an NMA record may link to multiple depicted places. The different denominators must not be combined.

Style classes were harmonised from institution-specific fields; AKON uses a binary colour field. Exact AKON dates exclude approximate or inferred periods. No finding measures historical postcard production, tourism, popularity or postcard circulation.

The downloadable data notes and summary-file index describe the supplied extracts and denominators. Collection totals describe this project snapshot, not current institutional holdings.
