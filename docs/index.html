Postcards of Australia

Places, Collections and Visual Culture

Historical postcards capture how places were documented and remembered.
This visual story explores Australian postcard collections through geography,
catalogue formats and recorded visual styles.

We begin with two Australian collections: NMA and SLNSW.
AKON provides an international perspective for comparison.

4. What Do These Collections Reveal?

Australian postcard representation is geographically uneven.
New South Wales has a particularly large presence in the collections examined.
Locality and hexbin maps reveal concentrations within that broader pattern.

Catalogue structure also shapes our interpretation.
Collections classify cards, grouped materials and visual styles differently.
Their percentages are comparable only with these differences in mind.

AKON provides a useful international contrast.
Its strong European concentration illustrates why archival coverage matters.
No single collection represents all historical postcards.

Data Sources and Methodology

Australian sources: National Museum of Australia, Josef Lebovic Gallery collection no. 1; State Library of New South Wales, Miscellaneous Australian Postcards.

International source: Austrian National Library, AKON Historical Postcards.

Geographic boundaries: Australian states data converted to TopoJSON; Natural Earth world boundaries. SLNSW locality matches and hexagons were prepared before visualisation.

Record counts measure collection holdings and metadata. They do not directly measure postcard production, circulation, readership or tourism.
